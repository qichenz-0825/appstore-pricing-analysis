Key findings

  • Freemium wins revenue. 99% of Top Grossing apps are free to download — money comes from in‑app purchases and subscriptions, not the upfront price. Lowest share: Medical (93%).
  • Charm pricing dominates. 98% of paid apps end in .99; the most common price point is $0.99, and the median paid app costs $3.99.
  • Category matters. Median paid price ranges from $1.99 (Social Networking) to $5.99 (Medical).
  • Price vs. rank. Pooled Spearman correlation between price and chart rank is -0.12 (pricier apps tend to rank higher).
  • Regional pricing. The same app is cheapest in Canada (index 94) and most expensive in United Kingdom (index 133) after converting to USD. Much of the gap is tax: UK/EU/JP/AU prices include VAT/GST while US prices do not. The UK sticker index of 133 falls to 111 once VAT is removed; tax‑adjusted, indices range from 90 (Japan) to 112 (Brazil).
  • Paid apps are a long tail. Median Top Paid app is updated every 133 days vs 7 for Top Free, with far fewer ratings — the developers with ad budgets are the freemium/subscription apps.

What this means for Apple Ads

  • Who buys ads: nearly all revenue-generating apps are free to download, so advertiser LTV comes from in-app purchases and subscriptions. Plan advertiser growth around freemium developers.
  • Category mix as a forecast driver: engagement share (ratings volume) sizes each category's advertiser pool; median paid price is a willingness-to-pay index for spend per advertiser.
  • International prioritization: compare markets on a tax-adjusted price index, not the sticker price, when sizing revenue per advertiser abroad.
  • These drivers feed reports/Apple_Ads_FPA_Model.xlsx (forecast, opex/headcount, budget-vs-actual, market NPV, sensitivity).
